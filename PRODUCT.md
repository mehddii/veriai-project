# Product

## Register

product

## Users
Primary users are students, professors, university reviewers, and research reviewers who need to assess whether submitted writing appears human-authored or AI-generated. They arrive with different levels of technical confidence, but they share the same context: they are reviewing serious academic or research material, often under time pressure, and they need the interface to help them inspect evidence rather than impress them with visual novelty.

## Product Purpose
VeriAI exists to help academic and research users review suspicious writing with clarity, speed, and defensible evidence. The product should make submission, analysis, and interpretation feel trustworthy and legible, especially when users need to justify a judgment to another person. Success means users can quickly understand the overall risk, inspect supporting signals, and feel confident that the interface is helping them review, not manipulate, the conclusion.

## Brand Personality
Credible, calm, forensic. The tone should feel composed, intelligent, and serious without becoming cold or intimidating. Users should feel that the product is structured like a reliable review instrument, not a hype-driven AI demo or a startup landing page dressed up as a tool.

## Anti-references
Do not make this look like a generic AI SaaS clone, a Stripe or Vercel derivative, a glassy futuristic dashboard, a crypto-trading interface, or a student LMS packed with cheerful productivity chrome. Avoid decorative gradients, startup-marketing hero templates, and any aesthetic that suggests trendiness, entertainment, or speculative tech instead of evidence-based review.

## Design Principles
1. Evidence before flourish. The interface should foreground interpretable signals, readable breakdowns, and review actions before any decorative treatment.
2. Calm precision over excitement. Visual choices should reduce anxiety and cognitive noise, especially when the content under review is sensitive or high-stakes.
3. Structured trust. Layout, copy, and state handling should make the system feel methodical and transparent, never theatrical or manipulative.
4. Academic seriousness without institutional dullness. The product should feel modern and polished, but still appropriate for classrooms, research settings, and formal review work.
5. Decision support, not decision theater. The UI should help users inspect and weigh evidence, not blindly accept a dramatic output.

## Accessibility & Inclusion
Target WCAG AA-friendly behavior across the authenticated product. The interface should support keyboard navigation, visible focus states, reduced-motion fallbacks, and strong contrast for all scoring and semantic states. Color should never be the only carrier of meaning for human, AI, or mixed classifications, and dense review surfaces should remain readable for users with varying visual comfort and technical fluency.
